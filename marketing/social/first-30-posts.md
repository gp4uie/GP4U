## 7. When to seek GP care — and when it's an emergency

- **Platform / format:** IG + FB · Carousel (6 slides)
- **Pillar:** GP education · **GP review required**
- **Hook:** GP, pharmacy or 112? A quick guide.
- **Caption:**

  > Pharmacy: minor ailments and advice on over-the-counter treatments.
  > GP (online or in person): symptoms that aren't improving, new or ongoing problems, prescriptions, certificates.
  > 112 / 999 or the Emergency Department: chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, signs of a stroke, heavy bleeding, collapse, a seizure, or a severe allergic reaction.
  > 
  > If you're not sure, don't wait — ask.

## 27. For parents: fever in children — when to get help

- **Platform / format:** IG + FB · Carousel
- **Pillar:** GP education · **GP review required**
- **Hook:** Parents: when is a fever a worry?
- **Caption:**

  > Fever is common in children and often a sign the body is fighting an infection. Get urgent medical help for a baby under 3 months with a temperature of 38°C or more, a child who is floppy, hard to wake, breathing fast or struggling, has a rash that doesn't fade under a glass, or seems to be getting worse. If in doubt, call 112/999.
