Walk-in is a good choice for

  • Coughs, colds, flu and chest infections
  • Sore throats and ear problems
  • Urine infections
  • Rashes and skin infections
  • Minor injuries and sprains
  • Stomach upsets and vomiting
  • Sick certificates and medical letters

What to bring

A list of any medicines you take, and anything relevant such as recent test results or letters from other doctors.

When to go to A&E instead

Call 112 or 999, or go to your nearest Emergency Department, if you or someone else has:

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Severe difficulty breathing
  • Signs of a stroke — face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech
  • Heavy bleeding that won't stop
  • A seizure or loss of consciousness
  • A severe allergic reaction
  • Thoughts of harming yourself — call 112 or 999, or Samaritans on 116 123
